# Competitive strategy — Google & Microsoft as the real gravity

We do **not** try to beat Google at maps or Microsoft at office AI.
We win a job they are structurally bad at.

## What Google optimizes for
- Help consumers **find** businesses (Maps, Search, Places).
- Sell ads and Business Profile completeness to those businesses.

Google will not ship: “here are restaurants that are *weak online* so a web agency can cold-email them under SPF/DKIM with human approval and suppression.”
That cannibalizes their own “complete your profile / run ads” motion.

**Our stance:** Places/OSM are **inputs**. Our product is **opportunity qualification + compliant outreach ops**.

## What Microsoft optimizes for
- Copilot inside docs/mail/CRM the customer already owns.
- Enterprise graph (M365, Dynamics, LinkedIn Sales Navigator).

Microsoft will not prioritize: local SMB website-audit signals + sending-domain trust gates for agencies in Dhaka/Delhi doing first-touch B2B email.

**Our stance:** Copilot drafts text; we own **lead fitness + send trust + audit trail**.

## Non-goals (refuse scope that feeds their strengths)
- Building a better Google Maps UI
- General sales CRM to replace Dynamics/HubSpot
- Generic “AI email writer” with no lead system of record

## Goals (where big tech is awkward)
1. **Opportunity score** from website/booking/contact gaps (rules, explainable)
2. **Human-approved** outbound with identity verification
3. **Org-scoped** suppression, audit, referral, billing
4. **Proof metrics** — connect/reply rates by vertical (publishable)

## Messaging
| They say | We say |
|----------|--------|
| “Search the map” | “Find businesses that need a site or booking link” |
| “Ask Copilot to write an email” | “Draft only after score + audit; send only after approve + DNS trust” |
| “Export places CSV” | “Export with provenance, score breakdown, audit timestamp” |

If we publish reply rates on one vertical and keep deliverability boringly strict, Google/Microsoft remain infrastructure — not substitutes.
